\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}
%%============================================================

Looking at 2D residual plots of $\hat\theta_{\rm best}-\theta_{\rm true}$
for the \code{confocal\_random\_zernike} (aberrated-wavefront) dataset, the
scatter looks Gaussian. That raises the obvious question: can a
Fisher-information / Cram\'er-Rao bound predict that spread analytically,
rather than only measuring it empirically?

This was tried once before and got stuck. From
\code{notes/joint\_profile\_inference.tex} (a sibling investigation,
\S2026-07-20): a Laplace approximation using the \emph{diagonal} of a local
finite-difference Hessian at $\theta$'s MAP was found \textbf{66--220$\times$
tighter} than the true empirical scatter across 50 shots. That work diagnosed
why, and flagged two things that were never actually done:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\theta$'s failure mode is a \emph{ridge} -- a continuous,
    weakly-identified direction (near-zero Fisher eigenvalue), not discrete
    multimodality like $\phi$'s. A ridge is compatible with local Fisher/CRB
    validity \emph{if} it is close to linear/quadratic near the truth -- but
    whether it actually is was flagged as unchecked (Future Work item 0a in
    that file) and never verified.
  \item The failed attempt used a \textbf{diagonal-only} Hessian, discarding
    exactly the off-diagonal correlations a ridge produces. \code{crb\_signal.py}
    already has \code{build\_joint\_fisher()} (full $17\times17$ joint
    Fisher over $(\phi,\theta_{z0},\theta_{z100})$, pixel-likelihood mode,
    all cross terms) and \code{full\_per\_shot\_covariance()} (inverts the
    \emph{full} matrix) -- but the latter was dead code, never called from
    that module's own driver. Nobody had tried the full-covariance version.
\end{enumerate}

This note finishes that specific, previously-flagged, previously-skipped
check, reusing the existing (but previously unwired) machinery rather than
re-deriving anything. Scope, by explicit direction: \textbf{one dataset}
(the aberrated \code{confocal\_random\_zernike} case), matching CRB to
empirical data. A Gaussian-beam-vs-aberrated comparison, and the downstream
connection to the $\beta$ (signal) CRB, are deliberately deferred.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%%============================================================

Dataset: \code{data/R20\_N200\_A1000000\_..\_psmapconfocal\_random\_zernike}
(20 runs $\times$ 200 shots, $10^6$ atoms/shot, aberrated wavefront via
\code{phase\_space\_grids.py}'s interpolated-PSMAP path). Cloud prior:
independent Gaussian on each of the 8 kinematic components (position,
velocity, position spread, velocity spread, in $x$ and $y$), $\sigma=10\,\mu$m
(or the matching velocity unit) for all 8. Evaluator geometry:
\textbf{exactly} what \code{non\_phase\_shear/analysis/generate\_kinematic\_estimates.py}'s
\code{best} (pixel-likelihood) fit uses -- \code{bins=32}, tight half-range
$1.54\times10^{-3}$\,m, \code{pixel\_n\_gh=8} -- so the CRB is evaluated on
the identical pixel/bin configuration that produced the empirical numbers it
is compared against. Using a different geometry would make the comparison
apples-to-oranges before it starts.

All CRB quantities below are evaluated at the \emph{true} $(\phi,\theta_{z0},
\theta_{z100})$ for each shot -- the standard Cram\'er-Rao convention, and
also convenient here since it needs no extra optimizer run (the empirical
$\hat\theta_{\rm best}$ values already come from a separate, already-run
fit).

Implementation: \code{python-scripts/theta\_crb\_vs\_empirical.py} (new),
importing \code{crb\_signal.py}'s Fisher-matrix code rather than duplicating
it.

%%============================================================
\section{Step 1: Is the ridge direction actually linear/quadratic?}
\label{sec:ridge}
%%============================================================

For 10 real shots (\code{run\_000}), built the full $17\times17$ joint
Fisher matrix $H$ at the true $(\phi,\theta_{z0},\theta_{z100})$, found its
smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ and eigenvector $v$ (the ridge
direction; $\sigma_{\rm ridge}=1/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}}$), and profiled the
\emph{real} Poisson NLL along $v$ at $k\sigma_{\rm ridge}$ for
$k\in\{0.5,1,2,4,8\}$, comparing to the Fisher-implied quadratic
$\tfrac12\lambda_{\min}(k\sigma_{\rm ridge})^2$.

\textbf{Result: not cleanly quadratic.} The ratio (actual $\Delta$NLL /
quadratic $\Delta$NLL) at $k=0.5$--$1$ (the scale that actually matters --
real fluctuations live near $1\sigma$, not $8\sigma$) ranged from
\textbf{$-2.9$ to $+4.3$} across the 10 shots -- including several shots
where the sign itself flips (the true NLL is briefly \emph{lower}, not
higher, than at the anchor point, in the direction the Fisher matrix calls
"increasing"). The ratio does converge toward 1 at larger $k$ (e.g.\ $0.80$--$1.23$
at $k=8$), consistent with the quadratic approximation eventually dominating
far from the anchor, but that is not the regime real shot noise samples.

This is a genuine, unresolved finding, not swept under the rug: the single
worst-conditioned direction is \emph{not} well-approximated by the local
quadratic at the scale that matters. And yet -- see \S\ref{sec:comparison}
-- the \emph{aggregate}, full 8-dimensional marginal covariance built from
the same local Fisher matrix matches the empirical scatter well. The most
likely reconciliation: $v$ (found by minimizing over the full 17-dimensional
$(\phi,\theta_{z0},\theta_{z100})$ space) is a mixed direction, not aligned
with any single physical $\theta$ component's own marginal-variance
direction, so a $2$--$4\times$ local mis-estimate of curvature along $v$
does not necessarily translate into a comparable error in any individual
component's marginal variance after the Schur-complement/inversion mixes
all 17 directions together. This is a hypothesis, not something checked
here -- flagged in \S\ref{sec:open} as follow-up.

%%============================================================
\section{Step 2+3: Full-covariance CRB vs.\ empirical RMSE}
\label{sec:comparison}
%%============================================================

For the same 10 shots, computed \code{full\_per\_shot\_covariance(H)} and
extracted $\sigma_{\theta_{z0}}$, $\sigma_{\theta_{z100}}$ (marginal std per
component), aggregated as root-mean-square across shots. Compared against
the empirical RMSE of $\hat\theta_{\rm best}-\theta_{\rm true}$, pooled over
z0 and z100, from the 13 completed runs of the real
\code{confocal\_random\_zernike} job available at the time of this check
(5200 shot/z-slice points).

\textbf{One data-quality issue found and handled}: the raw empirical RMSE
for $\sigma_{vy}$ was $2.33\times10^{-6}$, a $3.46\times$ mismatch against
the CRB's $6.75\times10^{-7}$ -- while every other component matched to a
few percent. Investigated directly rather than accepted: of 5200 points, a
single shot has a $\sigma_{vy}$ residual of $1.6\times10^{-4}$ ($161\,\mu$m)
-- roughly $70\times$ larger than the next-largest residual in that
component and wildly inconsistent with the rest of the distribution (median
$|{\rm residual}|=4.4\times10^{-7}$, matching $\sigma_{vx}$'s median almost
exactly). RMSE is quadratic in residuals, so this one likely optimizer
failure (an L-BFGS-B run landing in a bad basin for that one shot -- not a
CRB or model problem) was enough to dominate the whole-sample RMSE by
itself. Excluding it (a MAD-based robust-outlier filter, $>20\sigma_{\rm
robust}$ in any component; 1/5200 points excluded) brings $\sigma_{vy}$'s
empirical RMSE to $6.77\times10^{-7}$.

\section{The full estimator spectrum: \code{best} vs.\ \code{moments} vs.\ \code{null}}
\label{sec:spectrum}
%%============================================================

Everything so far compared the CRB against \code{best} (the full
pixel-likelihood MAP fit) only. \code{generate\_kinematic\_estimates.py}
actually produces three theta estimates per shot from the same image:
\code{best}, \code{moments} (a closed-form linear estimator, described
below), and \code{null} (the prior mean, using no image information at
all). Putting all three next to the CRB in one table answers a sharper
question than "does the CRB match the data": it shows \emph{where each
estimator sits between the two extremes of no information (null) and full
information (the CRB, an idealised efficient estimator)} -- and, since
\code{null} and \code{moments} both have closed-form theoretical
predictions of their own (derived below), whether an estimator's
under-performance relative to the CRB is understood \emph{quantitatively},
not just observed as a ratio.

The immediate pattern -- \code{moments}$\approx$\code{null} for the four

curiosity.

\subsection{Theoretical prediction for \code{null}: trivial, and exact}
\label{sec:null-theory}

\code{null} is \code{map\_eta} with the innovation forced to zero: it always
returns the prior mean $m_\eta$, using no per-shot information whatsoever.
There is nothing to propagate -- $\rm{RMSE}(\code{null})$ is, by definition,
the population spread of the true $\theta$ around the prior mean, i.e.\ the
prior width itself. Reading straight off \code{map\_inference.py}'s own
prior constants (no new code):
\[
  \sigma_{\mu_{x0}}=\sigma_{\mu_{y0}}=\sigma_{\mu_{vx0}}=\sigma_{\mu_{vy0}}
  = \code{TAU\_MU\_POS} = \code{TAU\_MU\_VEL} = 10\,\mu{\rm m}.
\]
For the width components the prior lives on the \emph{variance}
$V_{x0}=\sigma_x^2$, not $\sigma_x$ itself:
$V_{x0}\sim\N(\bar\sigma_{\rm pos}^2,\,\tau_{V,{\rm pos}}^2)$ with
$\tau_{V,{\rm pos}}=2\bar\sigma_{\rm pos}\,\code{SIG\_SIG\_POS}$ (\code{build\_prior}'s
own definition). The delta method for $\sigma_x=\sqrt{V_{x0}}$ gives
\[
  {\rm std}(\sigma_x) \;\approx\; \frac{\tau_{V,{\rm pos}}}{2\bar\sigma_{\rm pos}}
  \;=\; \code{SIG\_SIG\_POS} \;=\; 10\,\mu{\rm m},
\]
exactly (the factor of $2\bar\sigma_{\rm pos}$ in $\tau_{V,{\rm pos}}$'s own
definition cancels the delta-method's own $2\bar\sigma_{\rm pos}$ Jacobian
factor -- \code{build\_prior} was evidently written with exactly this
identity in mind). So the theoretical prediction is $10\,\mu{\rm m}$ for
\emph{all eight} components, uniformly. Table~\ref{tab:spectrum} shows this
matches empirical \code{null} RMSE to within $0$--$9\%$ across all three
datasets -- as it must, since by construction \code{null} carries zero
per-shot Fisher information and this is purely a statement about how the
data-generating prior is parameterised.

\subsection{Theoretical prediction for \code{moments}: linear Poisson-noise propagation}
\label{sec:moments-theory}

\code{theta\_moments} is \emph{exactly} linear in the raw image moments
$M=[\mu_{xf},\mu_{yf},V_{xf},V_{yf}]$ (\code{map\_inference.py}'s
\code{build\_A}/\code{build\_prior}/\code{precompute\_kalman}/\code{map\_eta}):
\[
  \hat\eta_{\rm moments} = m_\eta + K\,(M - A\,m_\eta),\qquad
  K = P_\eta A^\top (A P_\eta A^\top)^{-1}\ \ (10\times4,\ {\rm fixed}),
\]
so its covariance is \emph{exactly} $\rm{Cov}(\hat\eta_{\rm moments}) = K\,{\rm
Cov}(M)\,K^\top$ -- no finite differences needed, just the measurement noise
of $M$ itself propagated through this fixed linear map.

\textbf{Poisson statistics of the raw moments.} The port-summed pixel row
sums $n_i\sim{\rm Poisson}(\lambda_i)$, $i=1,\dots,{\rm bins}$, are
independent, with $N=\sum_i n_i$. This is the standard Poisson-thinning
identity: conditional on $N$, $(n_i)$ is \emph{exactly}
${\rm Multinomial}(N, p_i)$, $p_i=\lambda_i/\sum\lambda$. So, conditional on
the realised $N$ (what is actually observed and used), $\mu_x=\sum n_ix_i/N$
and $V_x=\sum n_i(x_i-\mu_x)^2/N$ behave \emph{exactly} like the sample
mean/variance of $N$ i.i.d.\ draws from the pixel distribution $p(x)$. The
standard (shape-agnostic) finite-sample results for a weighted
mean/variance estimator are
\[
  {\rm Var}(\mu_x) = \frac{V_x}{N},\qquad
  {\rm Cov}(\mu_x,V_x) = \frac{\mu_3}{N},\qquad
  {\rm Var}(V_x) = \frac{\mu_4-V_x^2}{N},
\]
where $\mu_3,\mu_4$ are the 3rd/4th central moments of $p(x)$, computed
directly from each shot's own measured marginal histogram (not assumed
Gaussian -- the PSF-blurred image marginal need not be exactly so). The
extra scatter from $N$ itself being random rather than fixed is second order
in $1/N$ (${\rm Var}(\mu_x)={\rm E}[{\rm Var}(\mu_x|N)]+{\rm Var}({\rm
E}[\mu_x|N])$, and the second term vanishes because ${\rm E}[\mu_x|N]=\mu_{x,{\rm true}}$
regardless of $N$) and is dropped. The $x$/$y$ blocks are treated as
uncorrelated (exact in the underlying cloud model, an approximation for the
measured image since both marginals come from the same shot's pixel counts,
but the leading-order terms above only depend on each marginal separately).

Evaluated on 20 real shots of \code{confocal\_random\_zernike}
(\code{python-scripts/moments\_null\_crb\_vs\_empirical.py}), this Poisson-only
prediction is:

\midrule

$\mu_{y0}$    & $1.8\times10^{-7}$  & $9.56\times10^{-6}$ ($53\times$) \\

4--8 orders of magnitude below what's actually observed.} At $10^6$--$10^8$

counting statistics alone for shot noise to matter; \code{moments}'s real
error must come from somewhere else entirely.

\subsection{Where \code{moments}'s real error comes from: shrinkage bias and PSF blindness}
\label{sec:moments-bias}

$A$ is $4\times10$: four raw-moment measurements cannot determine ten
$\eta$ components, so $K$ is a \emph{regularised} (prior-shrunk)
pseudo-inverse, not a true inverse. Even with a perfectly noiseless
measurement $M=A\,\eta_{\rm true}$ (no Poisson noise at all), the recovered
$\hat\eta = m_\eta + K A(\eta_{\rm true}-m_\eta) \neq \eta_{\rm true}$ in
general -- only equal in directions where $KA$ acts as the identity. This is
a \emph{bias}, not a variance, and it doesn't shrink with more photons
(unlike the Poisson term above). Computing this shrinkage bias directly,
$(I-KA)(\eta_{\rm true}-m_\eta)$, over the true $\theta$ values of the full
aberrated dataset (4200 points) and converting back to $\theta$-space:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Component & Shrinkage-bias-only prediction & Empirical \code{moments} RMSE & Ratio \\
\midrule
$\mu_{x0}$    & $2.56\times10^{-6}$ & $9.78\times10^{-6}$ & $3.8\times$ (too small) \\
$\mu_{vx0}$   & $9.71\times10^{-6}$ & $2.66\times10^{-6}$ & $3.7\times$ (too large) \\
$\sigma_x$    & $9.09\times10^{-8}$ & $1.00\times10^{-5}$ & $110\times$ (too small) \\
$\sigma_{vx}$ & $1.32\times10^{-6}$ & $9.15\times10^{-6}$ & $6.9\times$ (too small) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(y-components essentially identical by symmetry, omitted.)

For the position/velocity \emph{means}, this gets the right order of
magnitude (a few $\times$) -- $\mu_{x0}$ and $\mu_{vx0}$ come out swapped in
scale relative to a naive "read the diagonal of $I-KA$" expectation, but
that's exactly what's expected once $KA$'s off-diagonal structure is
accounted for: a single scalar measurement ($\mu_{xf}$) is trying to inform
\emph{two} unknowns ($\mu_{x0}$ and $\mu_{vx0}$) simultaneously, so $K$'s
corresponding $2\times2$ sub-block genuinely mixes credit between them
rather than acting diagonally -- not a bug, just a reminder that
$4\to10$-dimensional shrinkage doesn't decompose component-by-component.

For the \emph{width} components, the shrinkage-bias-only picture is wrong
by $100\times$ or more, and the reason is physical rather than
statistical: $A$ (\code{build\_A}) models \emph{pure ballistic expansion}
-- $V_{xf}=V_{x0}+2TC_{xv0}+T^2V_{vx0}$ -- with \textbf{no imaging
point-spread-function term at all}. The real detected image's second
moment $V_{xf}$ is inflated by the optical PSF on top of the true ballistic
spread, by an amount this idealised $A$ has no way to know about, so the
"exact constraint" $M=A\eta$ this whole propagation assumes is itself
wrong at the scale of the PSF -- a model-misspecification bias, not a
statistical-shrinkage one, and much larger than either term computed above.
This is consistent with everything already observed: \code{moments}'s width
recovery tracks \code{null} almost exactly (Table~\ref{tab:spectrum}) --
i.e.\ the raw second image moment carries essentially \emph{no} usable width
information once the PSF is present -- while \code{best} (which fits the
true PSF-aware forward model via the PSMAP, not the raw moments) recovers
width to the CRB. Quantifying the PSF-variance term explicitly to close
this last gap numerically is listed in \S\ref{sec:open}.

%%============================================================
\section{Framing: this is a single-shot comparison, and that's intentional}
\label{sec:framing}
%%============================================================

$\theta$ is a fresh, independent nuisance parameter drawn from the prior
\emph{every shot} -- it is not a shared, estimated-once parameter like
$\beta=(A_s,A_c)$. Consequently, pooling more shots does \emph{not} reduce
the true per-shot $\theta$ RMSE (there is nothing to pool: shot $i$'s
$\theta_i$ tells you nothing about shot $j$'s $\theta_j$). What more shots
\emph{do} improve is the \emph{measurement precision of the empirical RMSE
estimate itself} -- with 5200 points the empirical RMSE's own relative
uncertainty is $\sim1/\sqrt{2\times5200}\approx1\%$, tight enough to
distinguish the observed few-percent CRB agreement from noise. This is why
this comparison uses many shots (for a trustworthy \emph{measurement}) while
still being a fundamentally single-shot statement (the CRB itself is a
per-shot quantity, not one that shrinks with $N_{\rm shots}$) -- unlike
$\beta$'s CRB, which genuinely does improve with shot count
(\code{crb\_signal.py}'s \code{crb\_from\_j}/\code{simple\_scaling\_crb}).

\section{From $\theta$-CRB to $\beta$-CRB}
\label{sec:beta}
%%============================================================

\subsection{Method: propagating theta uncertainty into a phase-information number}
\label{sec:beta-method}

\code{crb\_signal.py}'s \code{per\_shot\_information} is exactly the
theta-uncertainty-propagation step: it takes the same joint $17\times17$
Fisher matrix $H$ used in \S\ref{sec:comparison} (over
$\phi,\theta_{z0},\theta_{z100}$) and Schur-complements \emph{only} the 16
theta directions out (not $\phi$), leaving a scalar effective per-shot phase
information $j_{\rm eff}$ -- literally "how much information about the laser
phase $\phi_i$ survives, after accounting for the fact that $\theta$ is
unknown and must be estimated from the same image." Since
$\delta\phi_i(\beta)=A_s\sin(2\pi f i)+A_c\cos(2\pi f i)$ enters only through
$\phi_i$'s argument at $z100$, the $N$-shot Fisher matrix for
$\beta=(A_s,A_c)$ is exactly $F_\beta=\sum_i j_i\,x_ix_i^\top$,
$x_i=[\sin(2\pi fi),\cos(2\pi fi)]$ (\code{crb\_from\_j}), and
${\rm CRB}(\beta)=F_\beta^{-1}$.

\subsection{Converting to rad/$\sqrt{\rm shot}$ and rad/$\sqrt{\rm Hz}$}
\label{sec:units}

Two useful normalisations, both already implemented:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{rad/$\sqrt{\rm shot}$} (a shot-count-independent number).
    When shots densely sample many signal periods ($\langle x_ix_i^\top
    \rangle\to\tfrac12 I_2$, true here: $f=0.3$ cyc/shot over 50-200 shots is
    15-60 periods), $F_\beta\approx(N j_{\rm bar}/2)I_2$, so
    $\sigma_{A_s}=\sigma_{A_c}\approx\sqrt{2/(N j_{\rm bar})} =
    \sigma_{\rm 1shot}/\sqrt N$ (\code{simple\_scaling\_crb}). $\sigma_{\rm
    1shot}=\sqrt{2/j_{\rm bar}}$ is what "rad/$\sqrt{\rm shot}$" means: it is
    the number such that the CRB at any shot count $N$ is
    $\sigma_{\rm 1shot}/\sqrt N$ -- report this number once, and it answers
    "what precision at $N$ shots" for any $N$ without recomputing anything.
  \item \textbf{rad/$\sqrt{\rm Hz}$} (an amplitude spectral density, the
    standard sensitivity unit in precision metrology). This requires one
    more piece of information this codebase does not define anywhere: the
    real shot \emph{repetition rate} $f_{\rm rep}$ (shots/second) -- every
    frequency in this codebase (\code{--signal\_freq}) is in
    cycles/\emph{shot}, not Hz, so there is no absolute time axis without
    it. Given $f_{\rm rep}$, standard white-noise-ASD conversion applies:
    \[
      {\rm ASD} \;[{\rm rad}/\sqrt{\rm Hz}] \;=\; \sigma_{\rm 1shot}\;[{\rm
      rad}] \times \sqrt{T_{\rm rep}} \;=\; \sigma_{\rm 1shot} /
      \sqrt{f_{\rm rep}},\qquad T_{\rm rep}=1/f_{\rm rep}.
    \]
    (Same logic as converting a per-sample noise std to a PSD/ASD for any
    white, sample-limited process: divide by $\sqrt{\text{sample rate}}$.
    Valid here because each shot's $\theta_i,\phi_i$ are drawn independently
    -- no shot-to-shot correlation to worry about.) $\sigma_{\rm 1shot}$ is
    reported below; multiply by your own $f_{\rm rep}$ whenever you have
    one.
\end{itemize}

Implementation: \code{python-scripts/beta\_crb\_vs\_empirical.py}. Uses a
representative sample of shots (not all $N$) to estimate $j_{\rm bar}$
(checked: relative shot-to-shot spread of $j$ is $\sim$9\% for \code{1e6},
tight enough that treating $j_i\approx j_{\rm bar}$ for all $N$ shots when
building $F_\beta$ via the real $x_i$ pattern is a good approximation, while
avoiding an $O(N)$-shot Fisher-matrix cost).

\textbf{Unlike $\theta$'s own CRB (which matched to $\pm5\%$), $\beta$'s CRB

and ruled out one concrete hypothesis before accepting this: rebuilt the CRB
using \emph{exactly} \code{beta\_fits\_from\_kinematics.py}'s own evaluator
geometry (\code{bins=16}, native half-range, vs.\ the tight/32-bin geometry
used elsewhere in this note) -- $j_{\rm bar}$ changed by $<0.3\%$
($1.523\times10^6$ vs.\ $1.526\times10^6$), so evaluator geometry is
\emph{not} the explanation.

\textbf{The actual explanation is structural.} \code{beta\_fits\_from\_kinematics.py}'s
\code{best} method is a \emph{two-stage plug-in} estimator, not the
idealised joint estimator this CRB models:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fit $\hat\theta_{\rm best}$ first (a point estimate -- whose own
    scatter matches its CRB, \S\ref{sec:comparison}).
  \item Convert to \code{crb\_signal.py}'s $\eta$ representation via
    \code{theta\_to\_eta}, which \textbf{hardcodes two cross-terms to
    zero}: \verb|[mu_x0, mu_vx0, mu_y0, mu_vy0, sx**2, |\textbf{\texttt{0.0}}\verb|, svx**2, sy**2, |\textbf{\texttt{0.0}}\verb|, svy**2]|
    -- exactly the position-velocity covariance terms.
  \item Fit $\beta$ from that fixed, cross-term-stripped $\eta$ -- $\theta$'s
    uncertainty has already been collapsed to a point estimate and its
    correlation structure discarded, not properly marginalised.
\end{enumerate}
This CRB assumes an efficient estimator that uses the full joint
$(\phi,\theta,\beta)$ information optimally (Fisher-matrix elimination is
the textbook-correct way to get the marginal CRB for \emph{an efficient}
estimator). The real pipeline throws away exactly the correlation structure
$\theta$ was shown to actually have (\S\ref{sec:ridge}'s ridge) partway
through. A two-stage plug-in estimator falling short of the joint bound is a
standard, expected effect, not a bug in either number -- the CRB is
correctly a lower bound; this particular real pipeline doesn't saturate it.

%%============================================================
\section{Phase-shear readout: a structurally different model}
\label{sec:phase-shear}
%%============================================================

Everything above uses the "kinematic" pipeline: two-port images fit
per-shot for the full 8-parameter cloud state $\theta$, with the laser
phase $\phi_i$ profiled out and $\beta=(A_s,A_c)$ fit across shots from the
$\hat\phi_i$ sequence. The \emph{phase-shear} readout
(\code{phase\_shear\_fit.py}) is a completely different measurement
strategy for the same underlying $\beta$: instead of resolving $\theta$ and
$\phi_i$ separately, it fits an 11-parameter Gaussian$\times$fringe model
directly to the \emph{ground-state} port image,
\[
  \code{model\_image}(x,y) = \exp(\log A)\cdot
  \exp\!\Big[-\tfrac{(x-\mu_x)^2}{2\sigma_x^2}-\tfrac{(y-\mu_y)^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\Big]
  \cdot\big[1 + C_{\cos}\cos(\cdot) + C_{\sin}\sin(\cdot) + \dots\big],
\]
independently at $z{=}0$ and $z{=}100$, via a plain (no-prior) Poisson MLE.
The fitted fringe phase $\phi_0=\arctan2(C_{\sin},C_{\cos})$ at each $z$
gives a \emph{differential} phase $\Delta\phi = \phi_0^{z100}-\phi_0^{z0}$,
which cancels the (unknown, shot-to-shot random) laser phase common to both
images and is the observable this pipeline actually uses to infer $\beta$.
This section asks the same question as before -- does a CRB predict the
empirical scatter -- for this structurally different model, built entirely
fresh (not reusing \code{crb\_signal.py}'s PSMAP machinery, since there is
no PSMAP forward model here, just a closed-form fringe fit).

\subsection{Fisher matrix and $\rm{CRB}(\Delta\phi)$}
\label{sec:ps-fisher}

At a fitted parameter point $p$ (11-vector), the Poisson Fisher matrix for
\code{model\_image} is built by finite differences directly on the
$n_{\rm bins}\times n_{\rm bins}$ pixel grid (cheap: no PSMAP lookup, just a
closed-form elementwise expression),
\[
  H(p) = J^\top W J,\qquad J_{k}=\frac{\partial\,\code{model\_image}}{\partial p_k},
  \qquad W={\rm diag}(1/\mu(p)),
\]
inverted with \emph{no} prior term (\code{phase\_shear\_fit.py}'s objective
has none), then delta-methoded through $\phi_0=\arctan2(C_{\sin},C_{\cos})$
using the $2\times2$ $(C_{\cos},C_{\sin})$ sub-covariance:
\[
  {\rm Var}(\phi_0) = \nabla\phi_0^\top\,{\rm Cov}(C_{\cos},C_{\sin})\,\nabla\phi_0,
  \qquad
  \nabla\phi_0 = \Big[\tfrac{-C_{\sin}}{C_{\cos}^2+C_{\sin}^2},\ \tfrac{C_{\cos}}{C_{\cos}^2+C_{\sin}^2}\Big].
\]
$\phi_0^{z0}$ and $\phi_0^{z100}$ are fit \emph{independently} (unlike the
kinematic pipeline's shared $\phi_i$), so
${\rm Var}(\Delta\phi)={\rm Var}(\phi_0^{z0})+{\rm Var}(\phi_0^{z100})$, and
the effective per-shot Fisher information for $\Delta\phi$ is
$j_{\rm eff}=1/{\rm Var}(\Delta\phi)$ -- feeding into \emph{exactly} the
same $\beta$-CRB machinery as \S\ref{sec:beta} (\code{crb\_from\_j}), since
$\Delta\phi_i$ plays the same role $\phi_i$ did there.

\subsection{rad/$\sqrt{\rm shot}$ for this pipeline}
\label{sec:ps-units}

The conversion is identical in form to \S\ref{sec:units} -- $j_{\rm eff}$
here is a differently-computed quantity (single-image delta-method
information about a fringe phase, vs.\ the theta-marginalised Schur
complement of \S\ref{sec:beta-method}), but once you have a per-shot $j$,
everything downstream is the same formula:
$\sigma_{\Delta\phi,\rm 1shot}=\sqrt{1/j_{\rm eff}}$ is the shot-count-independent
number (note: no factor of 2 here, unlike \S\ref{sec:units}'s
$\sqrt{2/j}$ -- that factor came from $\beta$ having \emph{two} free
parameters $A_s,A_c$ sharing one scalar phase information $j$ per shot;
here we're first reporting $\Delta\phi$'s own single-parameter CRB, one
number, before it separately propagates into the 2-parameter $\beta$ CRB
via \code{crb\_from\_j} exactly as in \S\ref{sec:beta-method}), and
rad/$\sqrt{\rm Hz}$ again requires the undefined shot rate $f_{\rm rep}$:
${\rm ASD}=\sigma_{\rm 1shot}/\sqrt{f_{\rm rep}}$.

\subsection{What "CRB vs.\ empirical" means here: the systematic-bias caveat}
\label{sec:ps-caveat}

\textbf{This is the key thing to understand before reading the numbers
below.} The CRB models \emph{pure Poisson shot noise for a model assumed to
be exactly correctly specified} -- i.e.\ it answers "if the only source of
error were photon counting statistics, and the 11-parameter fringe model
were a perfect description of the image, how precisely could $\Delta\phi$
be recovered?" The empirical results in
\code{phase\_shear/paper/phase\_shear\_results.tex} show that the
\emph{raw} $\Delta\phi$ residual has a large \textbf{systematic} component
on top of this -- a "wavefront-averaging effect" that depends on the
cloud's position/shape and does \emph{not} cancel between the $z0$ and
$z100$ fits -- which the published analysis regresses out using either a
fitted-feature correction (in-sample/out-of-sample) or an oracle
(true-parameter) correction. That regression removes most, but not all, of
the systematic, leaving a corrected residual that is closer to -- but still
not identical to -- the pure noise floor the CRB models.

So there are three fundamentally different numbers in play, and comparing
the CRB only to the \emph{raw} one would be comparing apples to oranges: the
\textbf{raw} residual (systematic + noise, the actual as-built pipeline's
current performance), the \textbf{corrected} residual (fitted-feature or
oracle, systematic mostly removed, the closest existing empirical proxy
for "how good could this get"), and the \textbf{CRB} (noise only, by
construction the floor \emph{under} even the corrected number, since a real
regression correction is never perfectly efficient). Reporting all three
side by side, rather than picking one, is the only honest way to present
this comparison.

\subsection{Empirical residual: a methodological trap, documented so it
doesn't recur}
\label{sec:ps-trap}

The exact definition of the published residual was reverse-engineered from
\code{phase\_shear/notebooks/shear\_mle\_distributions.ipynb} (via
\code{jupyter nbconvert}) because a first, naive attempt --
$\code{wrap}(\phi_0^{z100}-\phi_0^{z0}-\Delta\phi_{\rm true})$ -- gave
$\approx142\,{\rm mrad}$ for \code{1e6}, roughly $10\times$ the published
$13.84\,{\rm mrad}$. The notebook's actual procedure has two non-obvious
pieces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{sign flip}: the "estimate" of the injected signal is
    \emph{negated} relative to the naive $\phi_0^{z100}-\phi_0^{z0}$
    difference (a sign convention in how the notebook defines its estimator,
    not a bug -- but invisible unless you read the notebook's own code).
  \item The DC/mean offset (an overall constant, expected: $\Delta\phi_{\rm
    true}$ varies shot-to-shot with a nonzero mean baked into the injected
    signal generator) is removed via a proper \emph{circular}-mean unwrap,
    \emph{then} an ordinary (non-circular) mean-subtraction on the
    already-unwrapped values -- not a single naive wrapped subtraction.
\end{enumerate}
With the corrected formula, the reproduced number is $13.15$--$13.58\,{\rm
mrad}$ against the published $13.84\,{\rm mrad}$ (small remaining
difference presumably a different run subset, not a formula mismatch) --
confirmed correct. Implementation:
\code{python-scripts/phase\_shear\_crb\_vs\_empirical.py}'s
\code{empirical\_delta\_phi\_std}, with the reasoning documented in its own
docstring so this exact class of bug doesn't recur.

\textbf{Interpretation, directly answering "what is this comparison
saying."} Three separate, consistent findings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The raw pipeline is nowhere near its own noise floor} --
    $3$--$33\times$ off, and (crucially) the gap gets \emph{worse}, not
    better, going from \code{1e6} to \code{1e8}. This is the clearest
    possible signature of a systematic, non-shot-noise error: shot noise
    shrinks as photon count grows (the CRB itself drops $10\times$, from
    $4.1$ to $0.41\,{\rm mrad}$), but the raw empirical residual barely
    moves ($13.15\to11.55\,{\rm mrad}$) because it is dominated by the
    photon-count-\emph{independent} wavefront-averaging systematic, not by
    counting statistics. A shrinking denominator with a flat numerator is
    exactly why the ratio explodes at \code{1e8}.
  \item \textbf{Once the systematic is regressed out (fitted-feature or
    oracle), the residual drops to within $1.6$--$4.5\times$ of the CRB}
    -- much closer, and now in the same direction/range as every other
    "\code{best}"-style CRB gap seen elsewhere in this note
    (\S\ref{sec:beta-1e6}, \S\ref{sec:beta-1e8-aberrated}: real, structural,
    estimator-inefficiency gaps of a few $\times$, not systematic-bias gaps
    of $10$--$40\times$). This is the fair comparison: it says the
    phase-shear readout's \emph{achievable} precision, once a good
    systematic correction is applied, is a few $\times$ the fundamental
    shot-noise floor -- the same kind of gap the kinematic pipeline's own
    \code{best} estimator has relative to its CRB, not a qualitatively
    different failure.
  \item \textbf{The CRB is doing its job correctly in both cases} -- it was
    never claiming to predict the raw number (that would require modeling
    the wavefront-averaging systematic, which is a model-misspecification
    effect a Poisson-noise CRB fundamentally cannot capture, by
    construction). Its value is as the \emph{lower bound against which to
    judge how much of the raw error is systematic vs.\ irreducible}: here,
    at \code{1e8}, essentially all of the raw $11.55\,{\rm mrad}$ is
    systematic ($1.08$--$1.85\,{\rm mrad}$ of it survives correction,
    against a $0.41\,{\rm mrad}$ floor) -- so future effort is far better
    spent improving the systematic correction (or removing its cause) than
    on anything noise-related.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open questions / future work}
\label{sec:open}
%%============================================================

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reconcile \S\ref{sec:ridge} and \S\ref{sec:comparison}.}
    Check what the worst-eigenvalue direction $v$ actually projects onto in
    $\theta$-component space, for a few shots -- confirm (or refute) the
    "mixed direction, doesn't dominate any single marginal" hypothesis
    directly rather than leaving it as speculation.
  \item \textbf{Diagnose the $10^8$ gap} (\S\ref{sec:baseline}): check
    whether \code{fit\_theta\_best}'s L-BFGS-B is actually converging
    (\code{res.nit}/\code{res.success}, and/or a tighter \code{maxiter} /
    warm-start-from-truth spot check) at $10^8$ atoms specifically. If
    convergence is the cause, this is an estimator-quality issue, not a
    CRB-validity issue -- worth confirming directly rather than resting on
    the plausible-but-unverified analogy to the joint-solve's documented
    $10^8$ struggles.
  \item \textbf{Exact (not $\phi$-substituted) \code{1e6} baseline} and a
    dedicated apples-to-apples aberrated-vs-Gaussian comparison at matched
    $N$: the \code{1e6}/\code{1e8} baselines used here are convenient
    (already fit, no new compute) but neither is parameter-matched to
    \code{confocal\_random\_zernike} as closely as
    \code{data/R80\_N200\_A1000000\_..\_f0.3000} would be (exact $n_{\rm
    atoms}$/cloud-prior/shot-count match, already on disk, just never run
    through \code{generate\_kinematic\_estimates.py}) -- worth doing if a
    tight, controlled aberration-only comparison is needed later.
  \item \textbf{Connect to the $\beta$ (signal) CRB -- done}, \S\ref{sec:beta}--\S\ref{sec:beta-1e8-aberrated}:
    the aberrated dataset's beta-CRB is $10^{-4}\,{\rm rad}$ at $N{=}200$
    (vs.\ \code{1e8}'s $1.8\times10^{-5}$ at $N{=}50$) -- aberration alone
    costs roughly a $6\times$ worse phase-information floor per shot even
    before any estimator-inefficiency gap is added on top. The general
    aberration-vs-Gaussian quantitative bound (the \emph{original} motivating
    question) is now answerable in principle from what's already computed
    here; a dedicated matched-$N$ comparison (see below) would sharpen it.
  \item The current check used only \code{run\_000}'s first 10 shots for the
    CRB side (cheap: $\sim1$ minute for all of \S\ref{sec:ridge}--\ref{sec:comparison}
    combined). Widening this to more shots/runs would tighten the CRB-side
    estimate too (currently it isn't the limiting source of noise in the
    comparison, but worth checking as the empirical run finishes all 20
    runs).
  \item \textbf{Quantify the PSF-variance term for \code{moments}}
    (\S\ref{sec:moments-bias}): the width-recovery failure of \code{moments}
    was diagnosed \emph{qualitatively} (the ballistic-expansion matrix $A$
    has no PSF term, so $M=A\eta$ is itself wrong at the PSF scale) but not
    \emph{quantitatively} -- computing the PSF's actual contribution to
    $V_{xf}$ (from the PSMAP/optics forward model already used elsewhere in
    this repo) and adding it as a third bias term alongside Poisson noise
    (\S\ref{sec:moments-theory}) and shrinkage bias (\S\ref{sec:moments-bias})
    should close the remaining $\sim100\times$ gap for $\sigma_x,\sigma_y$
    and would make the \code{moments} error budget fully closed-form, not
    just qualitatively understood.
  \item \textbf{Explain why \code{moments}-$\beta$ beats \code{best}-$\beta$
    for the aberrated dataset only} (\S\ref{sec:beta-1e8-aberrated}): flagged
    as a real, counter-intuitive, unexplained result -- the working
    hypothesis (that \code{best}'s two-stage cross-term loss interacts worst
    exactly where theta's ridge is strongest) is plausible but untested;
    would need a direct comparison of the two estimators' $\eta$-space error
    vectors on the same shots to confirm.
  \item \textbf{The systematic-bias-vs-noise-floor caveat
    (\S\ref{sec:ps-caveat}) is a general principle, not just a phase-shear
    one} -- worth stating explicitly: \emph{any} CRB in this note only
    models the noise floor of a correctly-specified model; a large raw
    empirical/CRB ratio anywhere in this document (e.g.\ the aberrated
    dataset's $\beta$ ratios of $22$--$29\times$,
    \S\ref{sec:beta-1e8-aberrated}) should always be interrogated for a
    hidden systematic/model-misspecification component before being
    attributed purely to estimator inefficiency -- the phase-shear section
    is the one place in this note where that check was actually done
    end-to-end (via the published fitted-feature/oracle corrections) and it
    turned out to matter enormously ($33\times\to1.6\times$ once corrected).
    The kinematic pipeline's own large gaps (aberrated $\beta$, \code{1e8}
    theta widths) have \emph{not} yet been checked for an analogous hidden
    systematic -- currently attributed to estimator inefficiency and
    non-convergence respectively, but neither has been ruled out as
    partly systematic in the same way phase-shear's raw number was.
\end{itemize}
